Layers doing one job

Thrilla is the interface. Open components do the work underneath — and none of them ever see the key that spends your bitcoin.

Layer 01 · Interface

Thrilla — the wallet you see

Derives keys and signs on your device. The server only broadcasts what you already signed.

Layer 02 · Backend

LNbits — accounts & orchestration

Thrilla ships as an LNbits extension, inheriting multi-user accounts, auth, and device confirmation.

Layer 03 · Indexing

BlindBit & Fulcrum — indexing the chain

BlindBit precomputes tweak and UTXO data so scanning is quick; Fulcrum serves lookups. Neither sees your spend key.

Layer 04 · Swaps Soon

Boltz — on-chain ↔ Lightning

Non-custodial submarine swaps — a failed swap refunds on-chain with a key only your wallet holds.

Security
The server never holds your keys

A full server compromise still can't move your bitcoin.

🔑

Seeds never reach the server

Generated and held on your device. There's no seed column in the database to leak — and no one to recover it for you.

📱

Account + device confirmation

A new device must be confirmed by email before it reaches a wallet, so a stolen password isn't enough.

✍️

Local signing

Transactions, PayJoins included, are signed on your device. The backend can't author one for you.

👁

Scanning is opt-in

Background scanning uploads your scan key only: it can detect coins, never spend them, and you can turn it off.

None of that helps if the app itself is fake. The Android builds are sideloaded, so check the one you downloaded before installing it — verify a download →
