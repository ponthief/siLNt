What it does
Reusable addresses without the privacy tax

Reuse one ordinary address and leak your history; generate new ones forever and drown in bookkeeping. Silent Payments end that trade-off.

🔇

Silent Payments

Publish one address. Each sender derives a different on-chain output, so nothing links your payments — yet only you can spend them.

Reusable & unlinkable
✉️

BitMail

Hand someone alice@mydomain.net instead of a 116-character string. Resolves over DNS.

DNS-backed
🤝

Collaborative PayJoin

Pay another member with a transaction you both put inputs into, breaking the "one owner per transaction" assumption.

Within your circle

Plus: coin control with dust flagging · biometric or PIN lock, and a duress PIN that wipes this device's keys · Lightning and on-chain ↔ Lightning swaps coming.

Who it's for

Trust inside, privacy outside

Thrilla is built for a known circle sharing one LNbits instance: members pay each other by PayJoin, and everyone shows the outside world a Silent Payment address that's safe to publish. Your keys are your responsibility — no one can move your funds, or recover them if you lose your backup.

Why it matters
Privacy that holds up on a public ledger

Every transaction is permanent and public. How you receive decides whether that ledger becomes a map of your life.

⚠ Reusing one normal address
  • Every payment to you is clustered under one address, forever.
  • Anyone who has paid you sees your balance and history.
  • Posting it publicly ties your identity to all of it.
✓ One Silent Payment address
  • Each payment lands on a fresh output, unlinked to the rest.
  • Observers see unrelated outputs — your history stays private.
  • Publish it anywhere; only your scan key finds your coins.

No address reuse, ever

Share one code for years; reuse of the public address never becomes reuse on the chain.

Nothing to coordinate

No asking senders for a fresh address, no invoice trail showing who paid what.

Fewer signals to analyse

Clustering relies on address reuse and single-owner inputs. This removes both.

What this doesn't do Straight talk
  • It protects the receiving side. Coins you spend still carry their history, so coin control still matters.
  • It isn't a mixer — amounts and timing stay visible, even when the links don't exist.
  • Network privacy is separate: who broadcasts, and from which IP, isn't covered here.
  • BitMail lookups are ordinary DNS queries, visible to the resolver. The payment stays private.
  • Your keys are your responsibility — there's no password reset for funds.
How it works
Layers doing one job

Thrilla is the interface. Open components do the work underneath.

Layer 01 · Interface

Thrilla — the wallet you see

Derives keys and signs on your device. The server only broadcasts what you already signed.

Layer 02 · Backend

LNbits — accounts & orchestration

Thrilla ships as an LNbits extension, inheriting multi-user accounts, auth, and device confirmation.

Layer 03 · Indexing

BlindBit & Fulcrum — indexing the chain

BlindBit precomputes tweak and UTXO data so scanning is quick; Fulcrum serves lookups. Neither sees your spend key.

Layer 04 · Swaps Soon

Boltz — on-chain ↔ Lightning

Non-custodial submarine swaps — a failed swap refunds on-chain with a key only your wallet holds.

Security model
The server never holds your keys

A full server compromise still can't move your coins.

🔑

Seeds never reach the server

Generated and held on your device. There's no seed column in the database to leak — and no one to recover it for you.

📱

Account + device confirmation

A new device must be confirmed by email before it reaches a wallet, so a stolen password isn't enough.

✍️

Local signing

Transactions, PayJoins included, are signed on your device. The backend can't author one for you.

👁

Scanning is opt-in

Background scanning uploads your scan key only: it can detect coins, never spend them, and you can turn it off.
