Features
One address. Nothing to join up.

Reuse an ordinary Bitcoin address and you publish your history. Mint a new one every time and you drown in bookkeeping. Silent Payments end that trade-off — and the rest of the wallet is built to keep it ended.

🔇

Silent Payments

Hand out one address for the rest of your life. Every payer works out a different destination, so the chain shows unrelated outputs — and only your key knows they are all yours.

Reusable & unlinkable
🧊

Coin control

Every coin listed with its own label, and a freeze switch on each. You choose exactly which ones fund a payment, so two coins never end up in the same transaction unless you put them there.

You pick the inputs

Dust-attack defence

Tiny unsolicited coins are how chain surveillance links your wallet together. WhiSPa spots them and freezes them on arrival, so they can't be spent by accident.

Locked to your device

Biometric or PIN to open, and a separate duress PIN that wipes this device's keys instead of unlocking. Nothing spendable is stored on the server — what it does see, and when.

Duress PIN

What a dust attack is, and why the freeze matters. Someone sends you a few hundred sats you never asked for. It looks like nothing — but the moment you spend it alongside your other coins, you have signed a transaction proving the two belong to the same wallet, and whoever sent the dust has just linked them. WhiSPa compares every incoming coin against a threshold and against your own change, and anything small that didn't come from you is flagged and frozen automatically — out of the coin selection until you deliberately unfreeze it. A freeze you set by hand is never overridden.

On-chain
What the watchers actually see

Chain analysis works by clustering: find one address used twice and you have joined two payments, two counterparties, two moments in someone's life. A Silent Payment address is never the thing that lands on the chain, so there is no repeat to find.

What this does not do. Silent Payments break the link on the receiving side. They do not hide amounts, they do not hide the payer's own coins, and they cannot help you if you spend two of those outputs in one transaction — doing that publishes the link yourself, which is what coin control is for.
